\subsection{Does Interaction Help? (RQ1)}

Without interaction, every system answers at chance (\V{OpusNoneAcc}--\V{GptSolNoneAcc}\%). Twelve experiments in the tested world raise accuracy for three of the four systems: GPT-6.1-Sol reaches \V{GptSolOwnAcc}\% (paired gain \V{GptSolOwnMinusNone} points, 95\% CI \V{GptSolOwnMinusNoneCI}), Claude Opus 5.5 \V{OpusOwnAcc}\% (\V{OpusOwnMinusNone}, CI \V{OpusOwnMinusNoneCI}), and Qwen 3.8 Max \V{QwenOwnAcc}\% on its completed worlds (\V{QwenOwnMinusNone}, CI \V{QwenOwnMinusNoneCI}). The gain comes with a shift away from textbook physics: the canonical-pick rate falls from \V{GptSolNoneCanon}\% to \V{GptSolOwnCanon}\% for GPT-6.1-Sol and from \V{OpusNoneCanon}\% to \V{OpusOwnCanon}\% for Claude Opus 5.5. Because canonical physics always selects a wrong candidate, this gain cannot come from textbook knowledge. Gemini 3.8 Flash stays at chance in every arm that relies on a history (own world \V{GeminiOwnAcc}\%, gain \V{GeminiOwnMinusNone}, CI \V{GeminiOwnMinusNoneCI}), so learning from interaction requires a capability that not every frontier model has. The checkpoint curves (Figure~\ref{fig:curves}) show the same ordering within the episode: GPT-6.1-Sol rises from \V{GptSolCpA}\% before any experiment to \V{GptSolCpD}\% after twelve, Claude Opus 5.5 from \V{OpusCpA}\% to \V{OpusCpD}\%, and Gemini 3.8 Flash from \V{GeminiCpA}\% to \V{GeminiCpD}\%. No curve rises monotonically; every system dips after eight experiments, so more evidence does not steadily improve the agent's belief about the law.

\subsection{Is the Learning Specific to the World? (RQ2)}

A history from a sibling world never helps. It leaves every system near or below its no-interaction floor (GPT-6.1-Sol \V{GptSolSibAcc}\%, Claude Opus 5.5 \V{OpusSibAcc}\%, Qwen 3.8 Max \V{QwenSibAcc}\%), and its errors are not random: \V{GptSolSibPickSib}\%, \V{OpusSibPickSib}\%, and \V{QwenSibPickSib}\% of sibling-arm answers select the outcome predicted by the sibling world's law. A system that has learned from a history applies the law that history shows; given another world's history, it applies that world's law. The gain from interaction is therefore specific to the world explored, not to familiarity with the setting (own minus sibling: \V{GptSolOwnMinusSib} points for GPT-6.1-Sol, CI \V{GptSolOwnMinusSibCI}; \V{QwenOwnMinusSib} for Qwen 3.8 Max, CI \V{QwenOwnMinusSibCI}). Claude Opus 5.5 shows the same direction with a smaller gain (\V{OpusOwnMinusSib}, CI \V{OpusOwnMinusSibCI}).

Table~\ref{tab:condition} splits accuracy by equation. The two kinds of departure are equally easy to apply once the law is stated: law in text reaches \V{GptSolLawStructAcc}\% and \V{GptSolLawFieldAcc}\% for GPT-6.1-Sol, and \V{OpusLawStructAcc}\% and \V{OpusLawFieldAcc}\% for Claude Opus 5.5. They differ in how well interaction reveals them. Claude Opus 5.5 learns structural changes from its own history (\V{OpusOwnStructAcc}\%, from \V{OpusNoneStructAcc}\%) but not hidden fields (\V{OpusOwnFieldAcc}\%, against \V{OpusNoneFieldAcc}\% without interaction). GPT-6.1-Sol learns both to a similar degree (\V{GptSolOwnStructAcc}\% and \V{GptSolOwnFieldAcc}\%).

\subsection{Does Acting Matter?}

A fresh instance shown the agent's episode as a record of another agent's experiments does at least as well as the agent that ran them: GPT-6.1-Sol \V{GptSolObsAcc}\% against \V{GptSolOwnAcc}\% (\V{GptSolObsMinusOwn} points, CI \V{GptSolObsMinusOwnCI}), Claude Opus 5.5 \V{OpusObsAcc}\% against \V{OpusOwnAcc}\% (\V{OpusObsMinusOwn}, CI \V{OpusObsMinusOwnCI}). Two factors differ between these arms: who chose the experiments, and how the history is presented. The own-record arm separates them by showing the same record in the same format, introduced as the agent's own experiments. Authorship has no detectable effect: own record minus observer is \V{GptSolOwnRecordMinusObs} points for GPT-6.1-Sol (CI \V{GptSolOwnRecordMinusObsCI}), \V{OpusOwnRecordMinusObs} for Claude Opus 5.5 (CI \V{OpusOwnRecordMinusObsCI}), and \V{GeminiOwnRecordMinusObs} for Gemini 3.8 Flash (CI \V{GeminiOwnRecordMinusObsCI}). For current systems, the value of interaction lies in the evidence it produces, and that evidence serves a system that did not gather it as well as one that did. Whether choosing experiments matters, as opposed to having made them, is tested by an agent whose experiments are chosen at random, which we leave to future work.

\subsection{Where Does Learning Break? (RQ3)}

Told the law in text, without any interaction, every system that learns from interaction reaches high accuracy: GPT-6.1-Sol \V{GptSolLawAcc}\% and Claude Opus 5.5 \V{OpusLawAcc}\%, with the canonical-pick rate near zero (\V{GptSolLawCanon}\% and \V{OpusLawCanon}\%). These systems can therefore read the rendered scenes and apply a known law to them; perception and application are not what limits them. The limit is in forming the law from evidence. Law in text exceeds the own-world arm by \V{GptSolLawMinusOwn} points for GPT-6.1-Sol (CI \V{GptSolLawMinusOwnCI}) and \V{OpusLawMinusOwn} for Claude Opus 5.5 (CI \V{OpusLawMinusOwnCI}). Interaction thus recovers less than half of what knowing the law provides: \V{GptSolOwnMinusNone} of \V{GptSolLawMinusNone} points for GPT-6.1-Sol, and \V{OpusOwnMinusNone} of \V{OpusLawMinusNone} for Claude Opus 5.5.

The gap persists when the history is re-presented in a fresh context: law in text minus own record is \V{GptSolLawMinusOwnRecord} points for GPT-6.1-Sol (CI \V{GptSolLawMinusOwnRecordCI}) and \V{OpusLawMinusOwnRecord} for Claude Opus 5.5 (CI \V{OpusLawMinusOwnRecordCI}). The bottleneck is therefore not only keeping a long interaction in view, but turning observed outcomes into the law that produced them. This gap is largest for hidden fields in Claude Opus 5.5, whose own history leaves field questions at the no-interaction level although it applies the stated field law at \V{OpusLawFieldAcc}\% (Table~\ref{tab:condition}).

\paragraph{Exploration.} Agents do explore the dimensions that reveal a field: GPT-6.1-Sol uses all three start times in 32 of 33 episodes and all three start distances in 31, so failures on hidden fields are not explained by never trying a different time or distance.
