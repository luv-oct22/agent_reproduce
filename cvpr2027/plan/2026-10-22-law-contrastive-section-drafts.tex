Foreseeing is posed as a choice among four rendered candidates $\mathcal{C}=\{I_1,\dots,I_4\}$.
Each candidate is the outcome of the same experiment under a different law of the same family,
regime, and condition: the canonical law $\Phi_{f,\rho}$ and three sibling realizations
$\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3$ of condition $c$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C}=\{R(\Phi_{f,\rho}(s,a,\Delta t))\}\cup\{R(\Phi_{(f,\rho,c,\lambda_k)}(s,a,\Delta t))\}_{k=1}^{3}.
  \label{eq:law-contrastive}
\end{equation}
The same four images form one question for each of the three sibling worlds, with a different
correct candidate in each. A system that does not use evidence about the tested world can therefore
answer at most one of the three questions correctly, and textbook physics always selects a wrong
candidate. Candidate positions are permuted independently for every item, so chance is 25\%.

\subsection{Simulator, Items, and Renders}
For each world $\mathcal{W}$, a deterministic RK4 integrator implements $\Phi_{\mathcal{W}}$ under the
realized law, including any structural change and hidden coefficient field. Items reuse the scenes of
the world hierarchy: for each condition, we search its scenes in a fixed order and accept a scene,
with the shortest elapsed time $\Delta t\in\{0.4,\dots,1.5\}$\,s, when the canonical outcome and three
sibling outcomes are pairwise at least 20 pixels apart at $1920\times1280$ and stay inside the frame.
New in-plane launches are sampled only to complete coverage. Every image is rendered by the same
Blender pipeline from the stored state; the renderer never advances physics. The prompt states the
initial position, velocity, start time, and $\Delta t$ in text.

\paragraph{Identifiability.} The search also tests whether each hidden law is visible at all. Two
realizations, a mild logarithmic correction to the inverse-square law and a weak quadratic coefficient
field, never separate from the canonical outcome by more than a few pixels at any admissible horizon.
We exclude them from the exam and report them as not visually identifiable.

\subsection{Shortcut Controls}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Law-contrastive candidates.} Every distractor is a physically valid outcome under another
  law of the same condition or the canonical law, so no distractor can be rejected as implausible.
  \item \emph{Shared images across worlds.} The same item is asked for three worlds with three different
  answers, which caps any world-blind policy at $1/3$.
  \item \emph{Candidate permutation.} Labels are shuffled per item; only the host stores the answer map.
  \item \emph{Canonical distractor.} The canonical outcome is always present and always wrong.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evidence Arms}
\label{sec:protocol:ablations}
All arms answer identical questions and differ only in the evidence the system receives.
\emph{No interaction} ($B=0$) gives the rendered context and the experiment description.
\emph{Own world} adds the complete interaction history recorded in the tested world.
\emph{Sibling world} adds a history recorded in another realization of the same condition, which is
equally informative about the family, regime, and kind of change but describes a different hidden law.
\emph{Law in text} gives the hidden law and regime constants in text and no history.
We report three paired contrasts on identical questions: own minus no interaction (does interaction
help, RQ1), own minus sibling (is the gain specific to this world, RQ2), and law in text minus own
(how much is lost in forming the law, RQ3). We also report how often each arm picks the canonical
candidate and, in the sibling arm, the sibling world's answer.
